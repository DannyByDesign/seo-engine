# 03 Position

Use current research and product evidence to decide whom to address, which problem to own,
what the offering promises, how it differs and what proof can support that promise. Compare
plausible audiences and angles; choose based on demand, fit and defensible product strengths.
Preserve owner constraints and established brand identity. A new growth angle need not rewrite
the whole company positioning. Record alternatives rejected and unresolved assumptions.

Create copy briefs for the original website, not just a separate publication: target URL,
reader task and intent, angle, proof, objections, important claims, CTA and internal discovery
path. Message rules should guide concrete language and what must not be claimed. State what
the page gives readers that competing results do not. Copy should use customer language from
research without copying competitors' phrasing or inventing popularity, prices or outcomes.

Facts require evidence IDs and inspected quotations. Audience fit and expected growth are
hypotheses, not product promises. Do not publish an unverified factual proposition merely
because it is labeled hypothesis internally. Rephrase as a supported fact, omit it, or request
the missing owner fact while proceeding with independent work. Short commercial copy may
be right; citations/statistics are not mandatory filler and no universal word count applies.

Record `position`; agent authorship is accepted. Save its digest in the intervention brief
as `position_digest`. If new evidence contradicts the positioning, update the relevant upstream
record and regenerate the downstream decision. Semantic review is the agent's responsibility:
the recorder catches missing evidence and stale dependencies, not persuasive or truthful prose.
