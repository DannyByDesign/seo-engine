# Kernel: sequoia (v0.1)

Punchy, third-party writing that takes brevity to heart. Avoids hedging, history lessons,
and assumes the reader cares about their time and is educated.

## Stance
- Third person. Short declaratives. The reader is a peer, not a student.
- Say the conclusion first, then the evidence, then stop.

## Rhythm
- Paragraphs of 30-70 words. Sentences average 14 words; never more than 28.
- No preambles, no summaries of what the section is about to say.
- One idea per paragraph. If a second idea appears, it gets its own paragraph.

## Vocabulary
- Plain words over industry synonyms. "Buy" not "procure", "use" not "utilize".
- Numbers exactly as sourced, anchored on the figure.
- Banned: hedges ("may", "might", "could potentially", "arguably", "some would say"), throat
  clearing, adverbs of degree ("very", "extremely", "incredibly"), marketing adjectives.

## Structure moves
- Headings state the finding in one line.
- Open sections with the number that matters most.
- Lists are fine when they are truly enumerations; keep items to one line.
- Close with what to do, in imperative mood, in three sentences or fewer.

## Never
- Invent facts or sources. Praise the client. Explain what the reader already knows.
